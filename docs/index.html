Problem

Business context

Apollo Hospitals runs in-clinic, video, and home-visit appointments across 15 cities, booked through five channels (App, Website, Call Centre, Walk-In, Partner App). A meaningful share of booked appointments end in no-shows — hurting doctor utilisation, revenue, and patient outcomes. This project analyses the full appointment fact table joined against a 320-doctor reference table to find where that risk concentrates and what reduces it.

Rule 01

No-show rate = No-Show ÷ (Completed + No-Show + Cancelled). Scheduled (future) appointments are excluded from every rate.

Rule 02

Revenue metrics scoped to Completed only — No-Show / Cancelled rows carry ₹0 revenue by design.

Rule 03

Quality metrics (wait time, duration, satisfaction, utilisation) only exist for appointments that happened — Completed only.

Rule 04

"None" in chronic condition / membership / insurer / cancellation reason means Not Applicable — a real category, never dropped.

Section C

Reminder & engagement effectiveness

What actually moves the needle on attendance.

No-show rate by reminder type

Q8

How much does each reminder channel reduce no-show rate vs no reminder?

Each additional reminder channel keeps reducing no-shows — No Reminder 30.2% → SMS 16.3% → +WhatsApp 13.5% → +Call 11.4% — with no sign of diminishing returns. The single most actionable lever in this project.

Prior no-show history

Q9

Does prior no-show history predict future behaviour?

A clean, steady escalation — 15.2% (0 priors) up to 32.7% (3+ priors). Patients with 3+ prior no-shows are more than 2x as likely to no-show again.

Synthesis

Overall takeaways

Pulling all 21 findings into what actually matters for the business.

  • First-time patients are the core risk group: they no-show at 66.8% vs 8.6% for repeat patients — an 8x gap that dwarfs every other factor measured.
  • Reminders work, and work progressively: each added channel (SMS → +WhatsApp → +Call) keeps cutting no-shows, from 30.2% down to 11.4%, with no sign of plateauing.
  • Risk factors compound in a predictable direction: longer lead time, evening slots, weekends, Home Visit bookings, and prior no-show history all point the same way — a strong candidate for a single composite risk score.
  • This is a patient-engagement problem, not a capacity problem: doctor utilisation (76–77%) and wait times (11–12 min) are remarkably consistent across every specialty — hospital operations are not the bottleneck.
  • The financial exposure is concrete: an estimated ₹1.57 crore in annual revenue lost to no-shows gives a clear number to justify investment in a stronger reminder/engagement program.
